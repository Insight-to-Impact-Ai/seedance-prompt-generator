# Intake questions

Always one `AskUserQuestion` call with all four questions. Each question: 3–4 options, the recommended one first and marked "(Recommended)", each with a `preview` sketch so the user can compare. Tailor the options to the genre named in the brief. Below are the option sets that worked; adapt the wording, keep the shape.

## Q1 — Setting (header: "Setting")

Ask: "Where does the scene take place?"

Action / spy example:
- Casino service corridor — marble, gold sconces, gilt mirrors, muffled roulette; mirrors give a "spots the tail" beat
- Underground parking garage — sodium light, wet concrete, pillars; colder, Bourne-adjacent
- Rain-slick rooftop, night — neon bleed, searchlight sweep; most operatic
- Hotel suite, lamplight — someone already waiting in the chair; slow burn

Drama / grief example:
- The living room, her bed by the window — one location, light tells the time
- The kitchen, the bowl still full — the domestic detail does the work
- The vet's exam room — clinical white, stainless table; one extra location
- The back garden — carrying her out; exterior, weather available

Preview each with 3–4 short lines: palette, one physical feature, one sound, the emotional read.

## Q2 — The turn (header: "The turn")

Ask: "What's the moment the scene changes, and who or what does it happen to?"

Every option names a second party or a physical rule. An option like "he runs across a rooftop" is an activity, not a turn; "he clears the gap and a hand grabs his ankle from the fire escape" is a turn. The preview sketches setup → turn → payoff in three short lines so the user can see the whole shape.

Action / spy example:
- Disarm, hand to hand — a guard steps out of the mirror-line; three beats in ~3s, no gunfire; safest to generate
- The callback standoff — he asks a question at the table, the answer starts the fight, he asks it again standing over the man
- Foot chase / parkour — something is behind him (a searchlight, a door banging open); he clears the gap and the last shot shows what he was running toward
- No violence, pure tension — he sees the tail in the mirror, decides, and walks out through the kitchen

Grief example:
- He finds her — the discovery, and the bowl he filled that morning still full
- He carries her — wrapped, only the tail showing, out to the car
- He can't put her down — stands holding her while someone waits at the door
- He speaks to her — the habitual line arriving too late

Drama / everyday example:
- The third party — someone else's story is the real one (a nervous kid beside him, a friend in love with the waitress); the payoff is what he does about it
- The reversal — what he appears to be doing is not what he is doing; the reveal is in a prop or a wardrobe change
- The rule that holds — a line, a table, a circle he or the other party cannot cross, and the scene tests it
- The slow crack — one locked shot, one face, pressure that builds until it snaps

Whichever the user picks, write the turn in one sentence in the reply before writing the story.

## Q3 — Shot count (header: "Shot count")

Ask: "How should the [N] seconds be cut?" Options come from the base guide's table, with previews showing the math and the beat names:

- 5 shots, 3s each (Recommended for standard narrative) — setup → spot → strike → prop → exit
- 3 shots, 5s each — slow burn, long holds; recommended for grief
- 4 shots, ~4s each — when one shot needs a full movement arc (a gap jump) or a flashback intrudes
- 7 shots, ~2s each — trailer rhythm, one action per shot, highest risk
- 1 continuous oner — nothing to mismatch, boldest

Mark "(Recommended)" on whichever the guide's emotional-intent table points to for this genre.

## Q4 — Dialogue (header: "Dialogue")

Ask: "Does he speak, and how should it end?"

- One line, cool and short (Recommended for most) — at the turn, or into a cuff mic
- Completely silent — score, ambience, the face
- A line at the very end — the button on the scene
- Two-way exchange — costs about a third of the runtime; only with a second reference

## After the answers

State the duration you're using (default 15s) and any deviation from their picks in the reply, with the reason and the alternative offered.
