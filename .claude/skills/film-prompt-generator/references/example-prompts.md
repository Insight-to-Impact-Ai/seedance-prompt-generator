# Example prompts

Three original prompts built with the procedure in `story-patterns.md`, one per common engine. Read the one closest to the genre in hand before writing shots. Each opens with the three-sentence story it was built from, so you can see how the story became shots. `@image_1` is a single character design sheet in every case.

---

## The Valet — the reversal

**Story.** A valet at a black-tie gala is handed the keys to a silver Bentley by a man who doesn't look at him. He brings the car around the long way, to the side entrance, where the man's wife is waiting with one suitcase. He gives her the keys and the line, and she drives off while the man is still inside toasting.

```
FORMAT: 15s / 5 SHOTS / A valet takes a rich man's keys and hands them to the wife who is leaving him.

SUBJECT: @image_1. Face, head and build exactly as the reference. Valet on shift. Polite, unreadable, eyes down around guests. The only time his face shows anything is the last shot, and it is very small.

SECONDARY SUBJECTS:
- The Husband — late fifties, silver hair swept back, black dinner jacket, heavy gold watch, never looks at the valet.
- The Wife — late forties, dark hair pinned up, emerald column dress, one hard-shell suitcase, coat over her arm. Calm. Already decided.

WARDROBE @image_1: Black valet waistcoat over a white shirt, black bow tie, black trousers, black leather shoes, small brass VALET pin on the chest. Identical in every shot.

HERO PROPS: One silver Bentley key fob. It passes from the Husband to @image_1 in SHOT 1 and from @image_1 to the Wife in SHOT 4, and is never in anyone else's hand.

ENVIRONMENT: A private club at night, rain just stopped. (A) The front steps under a red awning, brass stands, valet podium, black cars queued. (B) The underground garage, sodium light, the silver Bentley alone in a bay. (C) A narrow side entrance off the alley, one bare bulb over a service door, wet cobbles, the Wife standing under the bulb with the suitcase.

MOOD: Cool and quiet. A small man does one loyal thing that nobody will ever thank him for. No triumph, no music sting.

MUSIC: Muffled string quartet from inside the club under SHOT 1 and SHOT 5. Garage hum and a single engine start under SHOT 2 and 3. Nothing but rain-drip and the idling engine under SHOT 4.

COLOR LOGIC: Black and wet-grey dominate, warm gold from the club windows and the awning. The emerald dress is the single accent and appears only at the side door.

STYLE: Ultra-realistic, modern drama, anamorphic widescreen, fine 35mm grain, practical lighting only. Slow motivated camera. Shallow focus on faces, deep on the wide.

LOGIC RULE: Only one @image_1 visible in frame at any time. No duplicates, no split screen, no multi-panel or contact-sheet layout, no white studio backdrop — a single live-action scene, not a character sheet. Same wardrobe across all shots. The Husband and the Wife never share a frame. The Husband never sees the Wife leave.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: No slow motion, no push-in on the final shot, no dissolves, no cut to black, no swelling strings, no smiling from @image_1 until the last shot, no on-screen text, no crowd at the side door.

---

SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:03, MS, 50mm, locked. (A)
The Husband comes down the club steps mid-conversation with another guest and drops the Bentley fob into @image_1's open palm without looking at him. "Bring it round." @image_1 nods once, eyes down.

SHOT 2 — 0:03 to 0:06, WS, 35mm, slow track. (B)
@image_1 walks the length of the empty garage under sodium light to the silver Bentley, thumbs the fob, the lights flash once. He gets in and the engine starts.

SHOT 3 — 0:06 to 0:08, MCU, 85mm, locked on the podium. (A)
The Bentley's headlights sweep the front steps from below and do not stop. The Husband, glass in hand, glances at the empty podium, then turns back to his conversation.

SHOT 4 — 0:08 to 0:13, MS, 50mm, slow track. (C)
The Bentley rolls to a stop at the side door. @image_1 gets out, walks round, and puts the fob in the Wife's hand. Quiet, steady: "He said to bring it round." A stated pause. "He didn't say where." She looks at him for a beat, then gets in.

SHOT 5 — 0:13 to 0:15, MCU, 85mm, locked. (C)
@image_1 stands under the bare bulb as the taillights leave the alley. The corner of his mouth moves, barely. He straightens his waistcoat and walks back toward the club. Hold on the empty alley as the frame settles.
```

## Last Bus — the rule that holds, with a callback line

**Story.** A man waits alone at a night bus shelter; a stranger arrives and stays on the far side of the yellow curb line. The stranger asks for a light, then asks again with his hand in his jacket. The bus arrives between them, the man steps on, and answers the question a second time through the glass.

```
FORMAT: 15s / 4 SHOTS / A stranger at a night bus stop asks twice for a light, and the bus arrives before he can ask a third time.

SUBJECT: @image_1. Face, head and build exactly as the reference. Waiting for the last bus. Tired, still, watchful. He never raises his voice and never moves faster than a walk.

SECONDARY SUBJECT: The Stranger — thirties, taller than @image_1, hood up over a grey beanie, dark rain jacket, hands in pockets, unshaven, eyes that keep going to @image_1's coat. Visually distinct from @image_1 in every way.

WARDROBE @image_1: Charcoal wool overcoat buttoned, dark jeans, black boots, a paper cup of coffee in the left hand. Identical in every shot.

HERO PROPS: The yellow curb line painted along the front of the shelter. The Stranger stays on the road side of it and @image_1 on the shelter side for the entire scene; nobody crosses it until the bus doors open.

ENVIRONMENT: A single glass-and-steel bus shelter on an empty suburban road, 1 a.m., light rain. One fluorescent tube inside the shelter, a timetable, a bench. Wet asphalt reflecting the shelter light. Nothing else lit for a hundred metres. The bus arrives from screen left in SHOT 4 and fills the frame between the two men.

MOOD: Held breath. A threat that is never named and never acted on, because the rule holds and the clock runs out.

MUSIC: None. Rain on the shelter roof, the fluorescent buzz, the distant bus getting closer through SHOT 3 and arriving with a hiss of brakes in SHOT 4.

COLOR LOGIC: Cold green-white fluorescent inside the shelter, black wet road outside. The yellow line is the one warm accent and is in every shot.

STYLE: Ultra-realistic, modern thriller, locked-off camera, 35mm grain, practical light only, shallow focus. No handheld.

LOGIC RULE: Only one @image_1 visible in frame at any time. No duplicates, no split screen, no multi-panel or contact-sheet layout, no white studio backdrop — a single live-action scene, not a character sheet. Same wardrobe across all shots. The Stranger never crosses the yellow line and never takes his hand out of his jacket. No weapon is ever shown.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: No weapon, no fight, no running, no shouting, no push-in, no slow motion, no lens flare, no other people, no on-screen text.

---

SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:04, WS, 35mm, locked. 
The shelter alone in the dark. @image_1 sits on the bench with the coffee. The Stranger walks in from screen right and stops on the road side of the yellow line. "Got a light?" @image_1, without looking up: "Don't smoke."

SHOT 2 — 0:04 to 0:08, MCU, 85mm, locked on @image_1.
He drinks. His eyes go to the Stranger's jacket pocket and stay there. Somewhere off screen a bus engine starts to be audible. He sets the cup down on the bench, slowly, and stands.

SHOT 3 — 0:08 to 0:11, MS, 50mm, locked, both men in profile with the yellow line between them.
The Stranger takes one step to the line and stops. Right hand deep in the jacket. Quieter: "You sure you don't have a light?" @image_1 says nothing. Headlights begin to wash across both of them from screen left.

SHOT 4 — 0:11 to 0:15, MS, 50mm, locked.
The bus slides in between them and fills the frame. Doors hiss open on @image_1's side. He steps up, turns in the doorway, and through the glass, flat and even: "Don't smoke." The doors close. Hold on the Stranger alone on the far side of the yellow line as the bus pulls out of frame.
```

## The Waiting Room — the third party

**Story.** Two candidates wait outside the same interview; the young one keeps dropping his folder and can't keep his hands still. The older man watches, says nothing, and when the receptionist calls his name he gives the slot away. The kid goes in, and the man sits back with nothing to do but wait.

```
FORMAT: 15s / 3 SHOTS / A man gives his interview slot to the shaking kid beside him.

SUBJECT: @image_1. Face, head and build exactly as the reference. Waiting for a job interview he needs. Calm on the outside, one thumb working the edge of his folder. His arc is small: he notices, he decides, he lets it go.

SECONDARY SUBJECTS:
- The Kid — early twenties, thin, first suit that doesn't fit, tie knotted too big, hair still damp, a cardboard folder he cannot keep hold of. Never speaks a full sentence.
- The Receptionist — heard and half-seen only, a voice from the doorway and a clipboard.

WARDROBE @image_1: Navy suit, white shirt, no tie, brown leather shoes, a slim black folder on his knee. Identical in every shot.

HERO PROPS: The Kid's cardboard folder. It hits the floor in SHOT 1 and its papers slide out; the Kid is still gathering them when his name is not the one called.

ENVIRONMENT: A corporate waiting area, mid-morning. Grey carpet, a row of four black chairs against a glass wall, a water cooler, a closed frosted door with a company logo blurred out, a wall clock. @image_1 and the Kid are two chairs apart. Nobody else is waiting.

MOOD: Quiet decency. No music cue tells us he's a good man; he just is, and only the Kid will ever know.

MUSIC: None. Room tone, the water cooler glug, the wall clock, the folder hitting the carpet, the frosted door opening and closing.

COLOR LOGIC: Flat corporate grey and glass. The Kid's blue tie and @image_1's navy suit are the only saturated colour. Cool daylight through the glass wall.

STYLE: Ultra-realistic, grounded drama, locked-off camera, natural performance, shallow focus, fine grain, no camera movement.

LOGIC RULE: Only one @image_1 visible in frame at any time. No duplicates, no split screen, no multi-panel or contact-sheet layout, no white studio backdrop — a single live-action scene, not a character sheet. Same wardrobe across all shots. The Kid and @image_1 are visually distinct: different age, build, hair, suit colour. The Receptionist's face is never fully in frame.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: No handshake, no hug, no smiling from the Kid, no music, no push-in, no dissolves, no on-screen text, no extra people in the chairs.

---

SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:05, MS, 50mm, locked, both men in frame.
The Kid's folder slides off his knee and hits the carpet, papers fanning out. He drops to gather them, hands shaking, one page stuck to his shoe. @image_1 watches from two chairs away, thumb still on the edge of his own folder.

SHOT 2 — 0:05 to 0:10, MCU, 85mm, locked on @image_1.
The frosted door opens off screen. The Receptionist's voice: "Mr. Hale?" A beat. @image_1 looks at the Kid, still on one knee with the papers. Then, level, not loud: "He was here first." The Receptionist, after a pause: "…Okay. Come through."

SHOT 3 — 0:10 to 0:15, MS, 50mm, locked, both men in frame.
The Kid stands with the folder against his chest, looks at @image_1, opens his mouth, closes it, and goes through the door. It shuts. @image_1 sets his folder on the empty chair beside him, leans back, and looks at the wall clock. Hold on him as the frame settles.
```
