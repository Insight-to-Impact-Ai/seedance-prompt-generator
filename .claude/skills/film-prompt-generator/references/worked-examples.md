# Worked examples

The two raw prompts this skill is calibrated to. Both were delivered as pages; both use a single character design sheet as `@image_1` (the second also uses a dog sheet as `@image_2`).

## Rooftop Gap Run — action, 15s / 4 shots

```
FORMAT: 15s / 4 SHOTS / An agent runs a rooftop line ahead of a searchlight and lands back into a walk.

SUBJECT: @image_1. East Asian man, early forties, slim athletic build, shaved bald head with close-cropped dark stubble at the sides and back, black rectangular-frame glasses. Trained, economical, unpanicked. Performance stays flat and focused throughout — jaw set, breathing hard, no smiling, no strain-face, no theatrics. Face, head, glasses, and build exactly as the reference.

WARDROBE @image_1: Black two-piece notch-lapel suit, jacket unbuttoned and flaring open as he runs, white dress shirt, black silk tie flicking over his shoulder, white pocket square folded flat in the breast pocket, black leather dress watch on the left wrist, black leather oxfords. Identical in every shot.

HERO PROPS: The black leather dress watch on @image_1's left wrist, used as a concealed cuff microphone in SHOT 4.

THE PURSUER: A police helicopter, never fully visible in frame — represented only by its sweeping searchlight beam, the shadows it throws, and rotor noise. No reference image, no pursuing characters on foot.

ENVIRONMENT: Adjacent city rooftops, night, after rain. Roof A is tar-and-gravel with boxy HVAC units and a low concrete parapet. A three-meter alley gap separates it from Roof B, which sits about one meter lower and is crowded with air-conditioning condensers and steel pipe runs. Roof B steps down again to a narrow service landing with a railing. Standing water on both roofs throws back the city glow.

MOOD: Total physical commitment without a trace of panic — a man solving a route at speed, who is still composed enough to be dry about it the second his feet are under him again.

MUSIC: Rotor thud and wind under everything. Low pulsing synth bass through the sprint. Everything drops to near-silence and wind for the flight across the gap. A hard percussion hit lands with him. Brass resolves into a cool walking groove on the last line.

COLOR LOGIC: Sodium-orange city glow and wet black rooftop dominate. The helicopter's cold white searchlight is the single accent, raking across the frame and separating him from the background whenever it crosses him.

STYLE: Ultra-realistic modern spy thriller, anamorphic widescreen. Fine 35mm grain. Practical lighting only — city glow, rooftop work lights, the searchlight. Stabilized handheld that stays with him, always at his level or slightly below. Deep focus on the wides, shallow on the closer framings. Real-time speed throughout.

MOVEMENT RULE: @image_1 travels left to right in every shot — screen direction never reverses. His momentum carries across every cut: he is already at full speed when each shot begins, never returns to a standing start, never stops, never hesitates, never resets his stride. Real human body weight and real gravity — he is heavy on every landing and absorbs it through bent knees and hips. The gap jump uses a single-foot takeoff at a full run, never a two-footed hop. The landing resolves into a diagonal shoulder roll across the back, not a head-over-heels somersault, and he comes out of it onto his feet without pushing off the ground with his hands. Arms drive in natural opposition to his legs at all times. His dress shoes lose a little grip on the wet gravel — fast, but not weightless.

LOGIC RULE: Only one @image_1 visible in frame at any time. No duplicates, no split screen, no side-by-side figures, no multi-panel or contact-sheet layout, no white studio backdrop — this is a single live-action scene, not a character sheet. @image_1 wears his black rectangular glasses in every shot, including mid-air — they never come off, never slip, never change shape. His head stays shaved and bald throughout, with no hair growth. Same wardrobe across all shots. The rooftop geography stays consistent: Roof B is lower than Roof A and stays lower, and he only ever descends, never climbs back up.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: No slow motion, no speed ramping, no wire work, no floating or hovering at the top of the jump, no superhero landing pose, no impossible distance or height, no parkour flips or somersaults, no gunfire, no blood, no on-screen text or titles, no fisheye, no lens flare, no smiling, no visible helicopter body, no crowd.

---

SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:04, WS, 28mm, tracking laterally, level with him.
@image_1 sprints left to right across the gravel of Roof A, jacket flaring open, arms driving in a steady rhythm. The searchlight beam sweeps across the roof behind him and keeps going past.

SHOT 2 — 0:04 to 0:08, WS to MS, 35mm, tracking with him across the gap.
One continuous movement. @image_1 plants his left foot on the parapet lip without breaking stride and launches across the alley, legs still cycling in the air, arms reaching forward. He lands on the lower Roof B, absorbs it into a diagonal shoulder roll, and comes up onto his feet still running.

SHOT 3 — 0:08 to 0:11, MS, 35mm, handheld, following close behind.
@image_1 threads between the condenser units and catches a steel pipe with his right hand, swinging his body around it and out the other side without losing a step.

SHOT 4 — 0:11 to 0:15, MS to WS, 35mm, tracking, then settling to a stop.
@image_1 drops off the ledge onto the service landing, absorbs the impact in a deep crouch, and rises straight into a walk. He lifts his left cuff toward his mouth. "I'm going to need the car."
```

Judgment calls made and flagged in the reply: 5 shots → 4 so the jump got a full 4s; helicopter kept off screen; jacket unbuttoned for movement.

## Carrying Rosie — grief, 15s / 4 shots, two references

```
FORMAT: 15s / 4 SHOTS / An old dog dies in her patch of sun and the man who raised her stands holding her, unable to put her down.

SUBJECT: @image_1. East Asian man, early forties, slim build, shaved bald head with close-cropped dark stubble at the sides and back, black rectangular-frame glasses. Gentle, contained, undemonstrative. Face, head, glasses, and build exactly as the reference.

SECONDARY SUBJECT: @image_2. Rosie, a senior golden retriever, fifteen years old. Deep reddish-gold coat, heavily greyed and whitened muzzle and brow, dark gentle eyes, long feathered tail and feathered legs. Thin through the hips with age. Coat, colour, face and tail exactly as the reference.

WARDROBE @image_1: Light blue cotton piqué polo shirt with the collar slightly turned up at one side, navy chino trousers, white leather sneakers. Black rectangular glasses. Identical in every shot, flashback included. In the present-day shots the left shoulder of the polo is darkened in a damp patch where he has been holding her against it.

HERO PROPS: A plain white cotton blanket, soft and slightly worn. A flat, hairless tennis ball, faded from years of use. A round tan dog bed by the window with the dent of a body still pressed into it. A rectangle of late afternoon sunlight on the wooden floor beside the bed.

ENVIRONMENT: The living room of a small, plain, lived-in house. Bare wooden floor, one large window, a worn sofa, a radiator. The dog bed sits by the window where the sun falls. One single location for the whole film — the flashback and the present happen in the same room, separated only by the light.

TIME AND LIGHT RULE: The light is what tells the two times apart, not a filter or an effect. The flashback is flooded with warm honey-gold late afternoon sun through the window, dust floating in the beam. The present-day shots are the same room under flat grey overcast light with the sun gone and the window dull. Same furniture, same angles, same room — only the light has changed. No colour filters, no vignette, no dissolves.

MOOD: The warmth of an ordinary afternoon, then the same room with the warmth taken out of it. A man holding on past the point where holding on helps, staying quiet the whole way through.

MUSIC: Room tone and a ticking clock under the flashback, with no score at all. A single soft piano note on the cut to the present. One more note as he lifts her. Strings never enter. The final seconds are room tone and unsteady breathing only.

COLOR LOGIC: The flashback is honey-gold and warm all the way through. The present is drained grey-brown — wood, walls, skin and the pale blue polo all desaturated. The reddish-gold of Rosie's tail is the only warm colour left anywhere in the present-day shots, and it is what the eye goes to.

STYLE: Ultra-realistic, A24 restraint. 50mm and 85mm, shallow depth of field, natural window light only with no artificial fill. Fine 35mm grain. Locked-off camera throughout — no push-ins, no handheld, no camera movement of any kind. Straight cuts only, no dissolves or fades. The stillness is the point.

PERFORMANCE RULE: Slow and small throughout — he never sobs. The crying builds in order and skips no step: jaw tightens, chin trembles once, breath catches high in the chest, eyes fill and hold for a long beat, then the first tear falls. Three tears across the whole film, no more. No wailing, no heaving shoulders, no hand over the face, no shaking, no collapse. He blinks slowly and holds still for long stretches. Tears run and he never wipes them away — both his arms are full. The line is delivered quietly and slowly with a clear pause in the middle, steady at first and cracking on the repeat.

WEIGHT RULE: In SHOT 3 and SHOT 4, @image_2 is completely limp and completely still. She is dead weight in his arms — heavy, unresisting, sagging slightly where the blanket is not supported. She never shifts, settles, breathes, twitches or moves in any way, and neither does the tail. @image_1 carries her low and braced against his chest with both forearms under her, the way a person carries something heavy they do not want to put down. His posture shows the weight — shoulders down, back straight, arms tight.

LOGIC RULE: Only one @image_1 and only one @image_2 in frame at any time. No duplicates, no split screen, no multi-panel or contact-sheet layout, no white studio backdrop — this is a single live-action scene, not a character sheet. @image_2 is alive and visible only in SHOT 1. From SHOT 3 onward she is wrapped completely in the white blanket with only her feathered tail uncovered, hanging down from the blanket and hanging perfectly still — no face, no head, no paws, no body visible, ever. The dog bed is empty in every present-day shot. @image_1 keeps his glasses on throughout and his head stays shaved and bald. He is alone — no other people, no vet, no second animal.

NEGATIVE PROMPT: No living or moving dog after SHOT 1, no dog's face or paws under the blanket, no wagging or twitching tail, no breathing or rising chest under the blanket, no second dog, no other people, no veterinarian, no medical equipment, no blood or injury, no sobbing or wailing out loud, no shaking shoulders, no slow motion, no camera movement, no push-in, no dissolves or fades, no cut to black, no swelling orchestral score, no on-screen text or titles, no lens flare.

---

SHOT 1 — 0:00 to 0:04, MCU, 85mm, locked. FLASHBACK, warm light.
Warm honey sunlight through the window. @image_2 lies in her bed with her greyed muzzle resting across @image_1's knee, eyes half closed, breathing slowly. His hand scratches behind her ear in a slow, absent rhythm. She sighs and settles heavier against him.

SHOT 2 — 0:04 to 0:07, MS, 50mm, locked. Present, grey light.
Straight cut. The same window, the same bed, the sun gone and the light flat and grey. The bed is empty with the dent of her body still pressed into it. The flat tennis ball sits where it was left.

SHOT 3 — 0:07 to 0:11, MS, 50mm, locked. Present, grey light.
@image_1 stands in the middle of the bare floor holding @image_2 across his chest, both forearms under her, wrapped completely in the white blanket. Her long feathered tail hangs uncovered below the blanket, absolutely still. He does not move toward the door. He just holds her.

SHOT 4 — 0:11 to 0:15, CU, 85mm, locked. Present, grey light.
Close on @image_1's face, the white blanket and a fall of golden tail still in the bottom of frame. The first tear runs. He looks down at the blanket. "I've got you." A long pause, and his voice goes. "I've got you, girl." He stays exactly where he is, holding her.
```

Backstory that went in the page aside (never in the prompt): Rosie was fifteen; her back legs had been failing for a year; she died in the patch of sun she'd slept in since she was a puppy while he was in the next room; he wrapped her in the blanket off the sofa to carry her to the garden and got as far as the middle of the room.
